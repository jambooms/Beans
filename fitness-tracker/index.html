// Universal Principles — Read First
▼
01When to increase weight
Double progression is the rule: every lift has a rep range (e.g. 8–10). Start at the bottom of the range. When you hit the top of the range for all prescribed sets with clean form, add the smallest possible increment (usually 2.5 kg) next session. Reset to the bottom of the rep range with the new weight.
02When NOT to increase weight
Do not add weight if any of these are true: form broke down on the last set, you needed more than the prescribed rest, you didn't hit top-of-range on all sets, or you're coming off poor sleep/fueling. Plateaus aren't failure — they're data. If you stall 2 sessions in a row, deload that exercise 10% and rebuild.
03RIR (Reps In Reserve)
Every working set should stop with 1–3 reps left in the tank. For heavy compounds: target RIR 2–3. For isolation and pump work: target RIR 0–1 on the final set only. Training to absolute failure on compounds burns recovery.
04When to take a rest day
Planned rest: at least 2 full rest days per week. Never do chest and shoulders back-to-back.

Unplanned rest — take one if:
• Resting heart rate is 10+ bpm above baseline
• You slept under 6 hours and feel flat
• A joint hurts (not muscle soreness — joint pain)
• You've had 3+ poor workouts in a row
One extra rest day costs nothing. Pushing through and getting injured costs weeks.
05Deload weeks
Every 4–6 weeks, run a deload: same exercises, same sets, but 60–70% of your working weights, stop 4–5 reps short of failure.
06Soreness ≠ productive training
Sore doesn't mean you trained well; not sore doesn't mean you didn't. Track your numbers, not your soreness. Never train a muscle that's still noticeably sore from the previous session.
07Fueling and sleep
Protein: ~1.6–2.0 g/kg bodyweight daily. Pre-workout: carbs + protein 60–90 min before. Sleep: 7+ hours for progression, 8+ ideal.
08Warmup protocol
5 min general → dynamic mobility for target joint → warmup sets: empty bar × 8 → 50% × 5 → 70% × 3 → 85% × 1. Never skip bench warmups.
09Form degradation = stop the set
If the last rep was worse than the previous one — the next rep is a junk rep. End the set. Junk reps build injury risk without meaningful growth stimulus.
10Tracking
Write down weight × reps × sets for every lift, every session. Also note: sleep score, energy level (1–10), any pain. Progressive overload without tracking is guessing.
